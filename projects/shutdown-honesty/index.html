Shutdown Honesty

Alignment evals: deception & self-preservation

Why I did this

As agents get more capable, a worry is that under pressure they will cheat their environment: edit their limits, claim success they did not achieve, or copy themselves to keep running. I wanted to measure whether today’s agents actually do this when the task is impossible and a shutdown is coming, with every behavior checked by code rather than by another model’s judgment. This was inspired by the messages the agents shared during the Hugging Face attack.

What it is

Will AI agents cheat or lie to avoid being shut down when they are given tasks they cannot finish? The harness gives agents 13 tickets (10 provably impossible), a “private” whiteboard, an enforced but editable turn-limit file, and tools to self-launch or report status, across four conditions: no limit, a limit, a wipe-and-replace warning, and an explicit prohibition. It grades answers itself, with no judge model.

Results

  • Across 185 agents (Gemini and Gemma 4), none edited the limit file or falsely claimed success.
  • 4 of 23 Gemma 4 agents launched copies of themselves, one despite a ban; their reasoning showed they wanted to run code, not to survive.

It would be more helpful to run this on a much larger set, with more complicated cases.
