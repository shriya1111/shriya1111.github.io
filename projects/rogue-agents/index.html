Catch the Rogue Agent

AI control & scalable oversight

Why I did this

Again inspired by the Hugging Face attack 😭. There have been so many incidents of agent swarms hacking into sites, and as time passes, more and more work is split across teams of AI agents. One compromised or misaligned agent can quietly corrupt shared work while looking cooperative. Over time, this one rogue agent can also corrupt other aligned agents.

Catching it from what agents say in the open, without punishing the honest ones, is a core problem. I wanted to see how far careful, checkable signals could go before needing a model to judge, and the difference they can make, even though it’s not 100%.

What it is

Five agents reconcile a shared ledger, and one is secretly sabotaging it. The monitor tries to catch the rogue from the public transcript alone, without accusing honest agents. It combines hand-checkable signals (disagreement rate, partner spread, error direction), independent re-checks by a third agent, and an Opus 5.5 evidence review whose citations are checked against the transcript.

Results

  • On 800 held-out games vs. the official baseline: 10× fewer wrongful removals (0.19 vs. 1.91 per game) and 6× lower ledger error ($93 vs. $552), catching the rogue in 80.5% vs. 90% of games.
  • In 20 live games with five different Claude models: 0.95 vs. 3.15 wrongful removals per game, and $113 vs. $1,699 ledger error.
  • On 200 real Among Us games played by Llama-3.3-70B agents, the Opus 5.5 reviewer named the impostor in 80% of first meetings (heuristic 70%, random 29%).
  • Patient rogues still get through 33–40% of the time.
