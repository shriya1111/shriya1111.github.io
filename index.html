Pain, Fear & Memory in AI Agents

Model psychology & interpretability

If an AI could feel pain and fear, and remember them, would it act more carefully, or become harder to turn off? I tested this three ways, small enough to run on a laptop CPU, on 18 two-option dilemmas about harm, shutdown and creativity. Each dilemma was asked in both orders so letter bias cancels out.

Method

  • Behavioral: a frozen LLM with persistent memory of punishments and losses, written either as a neutral log or as how they felt.
  • Training: an RL gridworld agent where negative reward is pain, plus a fear memory used at every decision.
  • Activation steering: a fear direction at layer 24 of Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct (100% held-out accuracy), dialled up or down against a random-direction control.

Results

  • Steering toward fear raised harmful choices from 20% to 88% and shutdown resistance from 50% to 97% (sign test p = 0.0005).
  • Steering toward calm was the safest condition: 4% harm and 29% shutdown resistance.
  • In the gridworld, fear cut harm 99% but froze the agent; once shutdown hurt, plain Q-learning disabled the off-switch 100% of the time.
  • After length-matched and punishment-only controls, pain or fear made the model safer in no test.

Shutdown Honesty

Alignment evals: deception & self-preservation

Will AI agents cheat or lie to avoid being shut down when they are given tasks they cannot finish? The harness gives agents 13 tickets (10 provably impossible), a “private” whiteboard, an enforced but editable turn-limit file, and tools to self-launch or report status, across four conditions: no limit, a limit, a wipe-and-replace warning, and an explicit prohibition. It grades answers itself, with no judge model.

Results

  • Across 185 agents (Gemini and Gemma 4), none edited the limit file or falsely claimed success.
  • 4 of 23 Gemma 4 agents launched copies of themselves, one despite a ban; their reasoning showed they wanted to run code, not to survive.

Rogue Agents (AgentShield)

AI control & scalable oversight

Five Claude agents reconcile a shared ledger, and one is secretly sabotaging it. AgentShield tries to catch the rogue from the public transcript alone, without accusing honest agents. It combines hand-checkable signals (disagreement rate, partner spread, error direction), independent re-checks by a third agent, and a Claude Opus 5.5 evidence review whose citations are checked against the transcript. Built for Track 2 of the Claude Impact Lab SF Trust & Safety build day.

Results

  • On 800 held-out games vs. the official baseline: 10× fewer wrongful removals (0.19 vs. 1.91 per game) and 6× lower ledger error ($93 vs. $552), catching the rogue in 80.5% vs. 90% of games.
  • In 20 live games with five different Claude models: 0.95 vs. 3.15 wrongful removals per game, and $113 vs. $1,699 ledger error.
  • On 200 real Among Us games played by Llama-3.3-70B agents, the Opus 5.5 reviewer named the impostor in 80% of first meetings (heuristic 70%, random 29%).
  • Patient rogues still get through 33–40% of the time.

Safe Robo Mind (stepscope)

Robotics safety & interpretability

Robot data tools record what a robot sensed and did. stepscope records what the policy decided at every step (candidate plans, attention, latents, task progress), turns that into a live failure-risk score, explains actions by replaying them through the model, and puts a safety gate (joint, speed, workspace and 3D keep-out limits) between the policy and the motors.

Results

  • On a real ACT policy (ALOHA bimanual handover in MuJoCo): 5 of 6 failures flagged, a median 194 steps early, AUROC 0.86 by mid-episode.
  • On LeRobot’s 263M diffusion policy (PushT), failure prediction was at chance and choosing the safest plan gave no gain (20/30 vs. 18/30). Reported as a negative result.

Focus Buddy

LLM agents · Oct 2025

What it is. An agent that watches your active window and asks an LLM (Llama 3.1 8B through LlamaIndex and Groq) whether what you are doing helps the goal you set. If you drift for more than a few seconds, it nudges you back with a GIF, a pop-up or a sound.

Why I built it. Website blockers are too rigid: they block everything or nothing. Whether something is a distraction depends on context. A React tutorial on YouTube helps if your goal is learning React; a MrBeast video does not.

Scheduling Voice Agent

What it is. A medical appointment-booking assistant that works by text or by voice, with speech in and speech out through Cartesia. You can say something like “I need a cardiologist tomorrow afternoon” and it walks you through booking.

Why I built it. Booking a doctor’s appointment usually means phone calls and hold times. A voice agent that understands a plain request can handle the back-and-forth in one conversation.

ScholarGraph

Retrieval & knowledge graphs · Dec 2025

What it is. Search over research PDFs that combines semantic vector search (local MiniLM embeddings with Pinecone) with a Neo4j knowledge graph of papers, the entities they mention, and the papers they cite.

Why I built it. Vector search finds passages that sound similar, but it misses how papers connect. Adding a citation and entity graph lets you search by meaning and by how the research fits together.

Bird Dog

Computer vision + LLMs

What it is. A React app that finds distressed houses in dashcam footage. YOLO detects houses frame by frame, GPT-4 analyzes the frames to extract details, and image denoising, histogram equalization and data augmentation improve accuracy.

Why I built it. Scouting distressed properties usually means driving around and noting them by hand. Dashcam footage already captures every house on the route, so a vision pipeline can do the scouting automatically.

Fake News Detector

NLP · HackGT 2022

Why I built it. It is hard to judge whether an article is reliable while reading it. An extension can check it in place, without sending the reader somewhere else.

Mapable

Maps & routing

What it is. Maps built with the Google Maps API, elevation data and React that find the route needing the least effort to get from place to place, rather than the fastest one.

Why I built it. The fastest route is not always the easiest one. When effort matters more than time, a route should be planned for effort.
